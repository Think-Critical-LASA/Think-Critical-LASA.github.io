59% of adults worldwide lack basic media literacy skills.
What does that mean? What is media literacy? How is it different from just… normal literacy?
Media literacy is defined as “the ability to ACCESS, ANALYZE, EVALUATE, CREATE, and ACT using all forms of communication.” Let’s break down what this really means.
ACCESS —> A basic media literacy skill is being able to find reliable sources of information. Using sites such as AllSides Media Bias Chart helps to identify how different news sites spin their stories to favor certain views. 
ANALYZE —> This is a major part of media literacy where many fall short. Being able to analyze media and understand it fully requires a few different thinking points. This can include things like: one, who benefits from this? Two, is this potentially harmful? If so, who is harmed by this? And three, what is the writer/speaker trying to convey? This is also where identifying spin, or how the story is told to favor a certain view, is most important.
EVALUATE —> To put it simply, this is the final judgement. Other words you could use would be either conclusion or opinion. No matter how you decide to define it, this final judgement should always be the product of your own thinking. Hence the  name of the publication, ‘Think Critical.’ This is the part other people/biased news stations try to do for you, so it’s important you can do it yourself.
CREATE —> Much like how being literate enables you to write for yourself, media literacy enables you to make content/communicate effectively. 
ACT —> As you know,  content on social media isn’t confined to the online space. It either is shared because of a real event, or it has an effect on events outside of the internet. Growing and training your media literacy skills will also help you discern truth from the ever-growing  mass of false or highly opinionated content.
